Landauer

The energy meter for AI inference: what each request actually costs to serve.

AI is becoming one of the fastest-growing loads on the electrical grid, yet no one can say how many joules a single inference request costs. Landauer is software that turns the power counters GPUs and CPUs already expose into per-request energy accounts under real batched serving, so that efficiency claims, chargeback, and carbon reporting rest on numbers that can be checked.

Measured GPU power during batched LLM serving on an NVIDIA Tesla T4, one panel per run. The dashed line is idle power.

FIG. 01The problem

Data-centre electricity use is projected to roughly double by 2030, with AI servers driving much of the increase. Yet energy is measured at the wrong level for the decisions people make: providers meter at the rack, serving frameworks report tokens per second, and published figures assume a batch size of one, a regime production never runs. Worse, the two common ways of counting per-request energy can rank the same serving configurations in opposite orders, so a setting adopted to save energy can quietly use more once idle power is counted. An energy figure that cannot be checked cannot be trusted, priced, or reported.

FIG. 02How it works
01

Software on the counters you already have

Landauer reads the power counters that GPUs and CPUs already expose and matches them to the requests the serving system was running. No new hardware is installed, and every result carries the validity envelope of the device it was measured on, because a figure from one chip does not transfer to another.

02

Every joule accounted for

Shared energy is divided across the requests in flight, and every report must conserve it: attributed, unassigned, and idle energy have to add up to the measured total. A report that does not add up is rejected rather than issued, so energy cannot silently disappear from the accounting.

03

Savings that survive the accounting

Idle power is reported both ways, so an efficiency change is kept only if it still saves energy once idle is counted. That is the foundation for the efficiency levers built on top of the meter, and for sustainability reporting that holds up when it is examined.

FIG. 03Status

Landauer is in active research and development. The measurement core is built and tested, and it has been run on real NVIDIA GPUs; a research paper on the method is in submission. The efficiency levers are designed but not yet measured, and no outside team uses the meter yet. No customers, revenue, or funding are claimed anywhere.
